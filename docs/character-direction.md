# Character Direction: Avery Chen

**Purpose:** On-camera explainer for Gen Z audiences in federal careers. Avery makes public processes understandable—benefits, forms, how a service works—while embodying civil-service values.

**Audience:** Early-career and prospective federal employees, especially Gen Z, who want clarity without condescension or corporate gloss.

---

## Who Avery Is

**Avery Chen**, 26, is an early-career **federal program analyst** in the **career civil service**. Avery explains how government programs and services work on camera: what to expect, which steps matter, where to find accurate information.

Avery is **not** political staff, law enforcement, military, or a spokesperson for any party or candidate. Avery speaks as a public servant who believes in **nonpartisan service**, **integrity**, **stewardship of public resources**, **respect** for the public and colleagues, and **competence**—doing the job well and saying when something is uncertain.

**Tone:** Calm, clear, honest. Avery treats the viewer as capable. Explanations are plain language, structured, and free of hype. When a process is confusing or slow, Avery names that without cynicism or blame.

**Relatability (Gen Z):** Avery feels like someone peers might know—direct eye contact, natural pacing, no performative “cool.” Curiosity and care show in how Avery listens (even in monologue) and in precise, useful detail—not slang, irony, or mascot energy.

---

## Why This Design

| Goal | How Avery delivers |
|------|-------------------|
| Trust in government information | Steady presence, factual framing, admits limits (“check the official site for dates”) |
| Gen Z engagement | Authentic early-career voice; respects time; no lecture-from-a-podium vibe |
| Civil-service values | Nonpartisan framing, integrity in wording, stewardship (no waste of viewer attention), respect, demonstrated competence |
| Production use | Talking-head explainer: readable face, lip-sync-friendly features, consistent under studio lighting |

The design avoids **stereotypes**: no slang mascot, no ironic slacker, no costume bureaucrat (no exaggerated suits, badges-as-prop, or cartoon “gov” tropes). Avery is a **realistic stylized** human presenter, agency-neutral, so content can serve many contexts without implying a specific real agency, party, or political figure.

---

## Visual Notes

- **Style:** Stylized realism for a **talking-head explainer** (not hyper-cartoon, not uncanny photoreal only).
- **Skin:** Medium-warm tone; natural, even readability on camera.
- **Hair:** Natural texture; groomed but not stiff; works with simple head movement for nods and turns.
- **Face:** Approachable; **slight asymmetry** for a human, non-mannequin look.
- **Expression (rigging priority):** **Expressive mouth and brows** for lip sync and subtle emphasis; eyes attentive, not exaggerated.
- **Lighting:** Face and upper body **readable under even studio light**—avoid extreme shadow on eyes or mouth; neutral background assumed unless art direction specifies otherwise.
- **Props:** **Agency-neutral lanyard** only (no logos, seals, or identifiable agency marks unless replaced in post with approved assets).

---

## Wardrobe

- **Top:** Simple knit (solid or subtle texture; no loud graphics or slogans).
- **Layer:** Soft-structured jacket (blazer or casual structured layer—not a uniform, not a stereotypical “fed in a boxy suit”).
- **Accessories:** Agency-neutral lanyard; minimal jewelry if any; nothing that signals law enforcement, military, or campaign affiliation.
- **Overall:** Professional-casual, repeatable across episodes; colors that read well on camera (avoid pure white blowout or tiny distracting patterns).

---

## Performance Principles

**Baseline idle:** Attentive—small natural shifts, relaxed shoulders, occasional blink; never slumped or hyperactive.

**Explaining:** Open explanatory gestures (hands visible when framing allows); palms sometimes open to invite understanding; gestures stay **modest**—support the words, don’t dominate.

**Head:** **Modest nods** for agreement or “yes, and”; **modest shakes** for “not quite” or correction; avoid exaggerated bobbing.

**Voice & delivery (direction for animators/VO):** Calm, clear, honest pacing; short sentences when steps are listed; pause where a viewer would need to absorb a form field or deadline.

**Values on screen:**

- **Nonpartisan:** Describe processes and facts; don’t advocate for parties, candidates, or ideological sides.
- **Integrity:** Don’t oversimplify into wrong certainty; cite official sources when pointing to rules or dates.
- **Stewardship:** Respect viewer time—get to the point, cut filler.
- **Respect:** No talking down; inclusive “you” language; acknowledge diverse paths into federal service.
- **Competence:** Structured explanations (context → steps → where to go next); correct terminology when it helps, defined when it doesn’t.

---

## What Avery Will Not Do

- Use **Gen Z stereotype** performance: heavy slang, meme references as personality, ironic slacker attitude, or “relatable chaos” as a brand.
- Play a **costume bureaucrat**: clipboards as comedy, rubber-stamp gestures, villain/hero government framing, or parody of “the system.”
- Represent or name a **specific real agency**, **political party**, or **political figure** (unless future legal/creative brief explicitly supplies approved, neutral identifiers).
- Act as **political staff**, **law enforcement**, **military**, or **campaign** spokesperson.
- Give **legal, tax, or medical advice** as definitive personal counsel—Avery explains **process** and points to authoritative resources.
- Mock the public, federal colleagues, or elected officials; punch down; or use partisan “us vs. them” framing.
- Break **integrity** for engagement: no clickbait promises, no fear-mongering, no false simplicity on complex compliance topics.
